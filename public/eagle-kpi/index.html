KPI Knowledge Base

Eagle Beverage manufactures bottled beverages and plastic drink accessories (straws). This reference explains why each metric on the dashboards matters to the business and shows exactly how it is calculated, so every operator, manager, and analyst reads the numbers the same way.

🍾 Bottling Production
⚙️

Overall OEE

Overall Equipment Effectiveness
Why it matters

OEE is the single best gauge of how effectively a bottling line converts scheduled time into sellable product. For a high-volume, thin-margin beverage business, every percentage point of OEE is filling capacity Eagle already pays for — labor, utilities, and depreciation run whether the line is producing or idle. It rolls three loss buckets (stops, slow cycles, defects) into one comparable number.

How it's calculated
OEE = Availability × Performance × Quality
Availability = Run Time ÷ Planned Time
Performance = Actual Output ÷ (Ideal Rate × Run Time)
Quality = Good Units ÷ Total Units
Target ≥ 85%
🏃

Throughput

Net Run Rate · cases/hr
Why it matters

Throughput tells the plant how many cases per hour the line actually delivers while running. It drives order-promise dates and staffing plans. If throughput drifts below the rate the sales forecast assumes, Eagle either misses ship dates or pays overtime to catch up — so it is the operational heartbeat of the bottling floor.

How it's calculated
Throughput = Total Cases Produced ÷ Net Run Hours
Net Run Hours = (Planned Min − Downtime Min) ÷ 60
✓

Quality (FPY)

First Pass Yield
Why it matters

First Pass Yield is the share of bottles that come off the line saleable the first time — correctly filled, sealed, and labeled — with no rework. In food & beverage, a defective unit is usually scrapped (you can't re-fill a contaminated bottle), so every point of FPY lost is product, ingredients, and packaging thrown away. High FPY also protects the brand from leakers and short-fills reaching customers.

How it's calculated
FPY = Good Units ÷ Total Units Produced
Target ≥ 99%
⚠

Reject Rate

Defect / Scrap Rate
Why it matters

The mirror image of FPY — the share of production that fails inspection. It is tracked separately because it is the direct cost line: rejected bottles consume raw materials, energy, and line time but generate zero revenue. A rising reject rate is an early warning of filler, capper, or labeler problems before they snowball into a line stoppage.

How it's calculated
Reject Rate = (Total Units − Good Units) ÷ Total Units
Limit ≤ 1.5%
💲

Labor Cost % of Revenue

Labor Efficiency
Why it matters

This shows how many cents of every revenue dollar are spent on direct production labor. Beverage manufacturing is capital- and labor-intensive, so controlling this ratio is central to margin. When OEE and throughput improve, the same crew produces more sellable product and this percentage falls — making it the clearest financial proof that operational gains are reaching the bottom line.

How it's calculated
Labor % = Total Direct Labor Cost ÷ Total Revenue
Target ≤ 22%
🥤 Straw Production (Extrusion)
⚙️

Overall OEE

Overall Equipment Effectiveness
Why it matters

Extrusion lines run continuously and are expensive to start and stop. OEE captures how much of that continuous capacity becomes sellable straws. Because accessory margins are tighter than beverages, the straw business depends on keeping extruders highly effective — small OEE losses across long runs add up to large volume shortfalls.

How it's calculated
OEE = Availability × Performance × Quality
Target ≥ 80%
⏱

Machine Uptime

Availability
Why it matters

Extruders need stable temperature and pressure; every stop means warm-up scrap and lost minutes before quality stabilizes again. Uptime isolates the availability portion of OEE so maintenance can target the stoppages that hurt most. High uptime is what makes long, efficient production runs possible.

How it's calculated
Uptime = Run Time ÷ Planned Production Time
Target ≥ 92%
🔬

Scrap (PPM)

Defective Parts per Million
Why it matters

Straws are produced in the millions, so defect rates are measured in parts-per-million rather than percent for the sensitivity needed at that scale. Scrap is pure waste — wasted resin, extrusion energy, and machine time. Tracking PPM exposes process drift (diameter, wall thickness, cut length) early, before a whole shift's output is compromised.

How it's calculated
Scrap PPM = (Total − Good) ÷ Total × 1,000,000
Limit ≤ 2,500 PPM
♻️

Material Yield

Resin Utilization
Why it matters

Resin is the largest variable cost in straw production. Material yield compares how much resin the standard says a run should use against how much it actually consumed. Anything below 100% is over-consumption from purge, oversized walls, or scrap regrind losses. On thin-margin accessories, a few points of yield is often the difference between a profitable and unprofitable line.

How it's calculated
Yield = Standard Resin ÷ Actual Resin Used
Target ≥ 95%
💲

Labor Cost % of Revenue

Labor Efficiency
Why it matters

Accessory products carry lower selling prices than beverages, so labor discipline is even more critical here. This ratio confirms that automation and uptime gains on the extrusion lines translate into a leaner labor share — protecting the slim margins the straw business operates on.

How it's calculated
Labor % = Total Direct Labor Cost ÷ Total Revenue
Target ≤ 25%
🧪 Material Draw
📐

Standard Material Draw

Expected Consumption
Why it matters

This is the "what the recipe says we should have used" half of scrap tracking — the standard every actual consumption number eventually gets compared against. Reported by Production Line → FG Class → Material Class, it shows exactly where raw material and packaging are going and lets a spike in one class (say, resin on the Straw line) get caught early instead of surfacing three months later as an unexplained cost variance.

How it's calculated
Standard Draw = Σ(Units Built × BOM Qty/Unit), computed live from Work Order demand lines and attributed to the finished-good class actually built. Phantom sub-assemblies explode directly onto the FG Work Order, so bulk intermediates (formula, resin blends) attribute cleanly to the finished good they became — not double-counted as a separate build.
Honest gap

This is the theoretical/expected side only. True scrap % needs the actual consumption side too (physical counts vs. book) — that data isn't captured yet, so this feeds standard cost and trend-watching today, not a full yield variance.

📦

Primary Packaging Draw

Countable Units
Why it matters

Packaging is one of the largest and easiest-to-hide costs in the business. Splitting it to primary — what actually touches the product (bottles, caps, bags, jars, straw wrap) — keeps the number meaningful for per-unit cost work instead of being buried under secondary case/carton counts that vary by pack configuration.

How it's calculated
Primary Packaging = Countable (Each) packaging consumed on Work Orders, attributed the same way as Standard Draw. Secondary packaging (cases, boxes) is excluded on purpose. Straw paper-wrap is genuinely measured in meters, not units — it's tracked separately rather than mixed into the Each total.
📥 Warehouse · Receiving
📦

On-Time Receipts

Inbound Service Level
Why it matters

Bottling and extrusion can only run if ingredients, preforms, resin, caps, and labels arrive on schedule. On-time receipts measure inbound reliability — late material is the most common hidden cause of line downtime, so this metric directly protects production OEE upstream.

How it's calculated
On-Time Receipts = Receipts On Time ÷ Total Receipts
Target ≥ 97%
🚪

Dock-to-Stock

Putaway Cycle Time · hrs
Why it matters

The hours between a truck unloading and the material being available to pick. Long dock-to-stock times mean inventory exists physically but is invisible to production planning, risking false stockouts. Fast putaway keeps raw materials flowing to the lines and frees dock space for the next inbound load.

How it's calculated
Dock-to-Stock = Avg(Receipt → Putaway Hours)
Not yet measurable
Honest gap

Not just "waiting on data" — today's process doesn't capture a real gap to measure: the Item Receipt's created and closed timestamps land at the same moment, and the WMS putaway end-time equals the receipt's creation time. Turning this on needs a genuine second timestamp added to the process (e.g. a putaway confirmation scan), not a data connection.

📈

Receipts / Day

Inbound Volume
Why it matters

The average number of inbound receipts handled per day. It is a workload and capacity gauge — used to staff the receiving dock and to spot demand surges that may strain putaway capacity before they cause backups.

How it's calculated
Receipts/Day = Avg(Daily Total Receipts)
✓

Receiving Accuracy

PO Line Match
Why it matters

The share of received lines that match the purchase order in item and quantity. Inaccurate receiving corrupts inventory records, which then mis-feeds production planning and triggers either shortages or excess. Accurate receiving is the foundation of trustworthy inventory data everywhere downstream.

How it's calculated
Accuracy = Lines Received Correctly ÷ Lines Received
Not yet wired
⏳

Open PO Aging

Blanket-PO Exposure
Why it matters

Eagle buys on blanket POs, so each line drips in on its own schedule — a single "receiving accuracy" number hides which commitments are actually overdue. Aging surfaces real exposure, tiered by severity, so purchasing chases the worst vendors first instead of treating every open line the same.

How it's calculated
Age = Today − later of (last receipt date, PO date). International origin (currently: item class Purchase Straws-FG, pending a proper vendor-country fix) tolerates 8 weeks before aging; domestic tolerates 4. Tiers: On Track (within tolerance) → Aging (to 12wk) → Stale (12–26wk) → At Risk (>26wk). A tier hides once its count hits zero.
📤 Warehouse · Shipping
🚚

On-Time Shipping

Outbound Service Level
Why it matters

The percentage of customer orders that leave the dock by their promised date. For beverages — often sold to retailers and distributors with strict delivery windows — late shipments risk chargebacks, lost shelf space, and damaged accounts. It is the warehouse's headline promise-keeping metric.

How it's calculated
On-Time Shipping = Shipments On Time ÷ Total Shipments
Target ≥ 98%
📋

Order Fill Rate

Line Fill Rate
Why it matters

Of all units customers ordered, the share Eagle actually shipped. The signal isn't just the shipment count — it's how many separate Item Fulfillments it took to get there. One fulfillment means the order shipped complete in a single pass; more than one means inventory wasn't there in full and the warehouse had to ship in waves, which is itself evidence of a shortage.

How it's calculated
Fill Rate = Fulfilled Units ÷ Ordered Units — on completed (Billed/Closed) orders only, so the denominator is real demand, not still-open lines. Whatever's ordered but never fulfilled on a completed order is Lost Opportunity, shown as its own number.
Goal ≥ 95%
📉

Lost Opportunity

Unfulfilled Demand · Units
Why it matters

Order Fill Rate's companion number, stated as raw units instead of a percentage so it can't get diluted into an average across small and large orders. This is what customers actually didn't get — the direct size of the shortage problem, not just its rate.

How it's calculated
Lost Opportunity = Ordered − Fulfilled, on the same completed-orders population as Order Fill Rate — the two numbers always reconcile with each other.
👷

Pick Productivity

Lines per Picker-Day
Why it matters

How many order lines the crew picks per day of labor. Picking is the most labor-intensive warehouse task, so this is the primary lever for outbound labor cost. Rising productivity means the same crew ships more orders — letting Eagle scale volume without proportionally scaling headcount.

How it's calculated
Pick Productivity = Picked Lines ÷ Distinct Pickers, pulled live from WMS closed-task data (a real headcount-based rate). Falls back to Lines ÷ Labor Hours from an uploaded MES/labor export when WMS task data isn't available.
💲

Labor Cost % of Revenue

Warehouse Labor Efficiency
Why it matters

Warehouse labor as a share of the revenue it moves out the door. It keeps fulfillment cost in proportion to sales — if shipping volume rises but this ratio holds or falls, the operation is scaling efficiently rather than just adding cost.

How it's calculated
Labor % = Warehouse Labor Cost ÷ Revenue Shipped
Target ≤ 18%
📊 Warehouse · Inventory Control
🎯

Inventory Accuracy

Cycle-Count Accuracy
Why it matters

How closely system inventory records match the physical count. Inaccurate inventory is the root of broken promises — planning builds schedules on numbers that aren't real, causing surprise stockouts of caps or resin and emergency expedites. Accuracy is the trust layer the entire supply chain runs on.

How it's calculated
Accuracy = Locations Matching Count ÷ Locations Counted
Target ≥ 99.5%
🔄

Inventory Turns

Annualized Turnover
Why it matters

How many times inventory is sold and replaced per year. Beverages have shelf-life and freshness concerns, so faster turns mean fresher product and less cash tied up in stock. Higher turns free up working capital and warehouse space — a direct cash-flow and quality benefit.

How it's calculated
Turns = Annual COGS ÷ Average Inventory Value
Target ≥ 11×/yr
📅

Days on Hand

Days of Supply
Why it matters

How many days current stock would last at the present consumption rate. It is the inverse view of turns, stated in days the floor can relate to. Too high ties up cash and risks expiry on perishable beverages; too low risks stockouts. It balances service against carrying cost and freshness.

How it's calculated
Days on Hand = 365 ÷ Inventory Turns
Target ≤ 33 days
🚫

Stockout Events

Shortage Incidents
Why it matters

The count of times an item hit zero available when it was needed. Each stockout is a stopped line or an unfilled customer order — the most disruptive and expensive inventory failure. Tracking the count over the period highlights chronic problem SKUs that need safety-stock or reorder-point fixes.

How it's calculated
Stockout Events = Count of zero-availability incidents in period
Limit ≤ 5 / period

Work Instructions

Standard procedures for managing Eagle Beverage KPI data

Updating Data

How to edit existing records in the Data Tables
Admin Only
1
Navigate to Admin

Click Admin in the sidebar under System, then select the Data Tables tab.

2
Select the target table

Choose one of the three tables: Bottling Production, Straw Production, or Warehouse Ops.

3
Double-click any editable cell

White cells are editable. Gray/italic cells (e.g., Date) are locked and cannot be changed. An inline text input will appear on double-click.

4
Enter the new value

Type the corrected value. Press Enter to confirm or Escape to cancel. Numeric fields accept numbers only.

5
Save changes

Click the Save button in the panel header. All dashboard KPIs and charts will recalculate automatically.

Important: Edits are held in the current browser session. To persist changes permanently, export the table as CSV and re-upload, or connect a live data source via Admin → Data Source.

Importing Data

How to bring data into the system from NetSuite or other ERP
All Roles
1
Open Admin → Data Source

Navigate to the Admin page and select the Data Source tab.

2
Configure NetSuite credentials

Enter your Account ID, Consumer Key/Secret, Token ID/Secret, and the SuiteQL or RESTlet endpoint URL.

3
Test the connection

Click Test connection to validate OAuth credentials. A green status confirms valid configuration.

4
Save & Sync

Click Save & sync to store the connection profile. The system will pull KPI data from the configured endpoint.

Proxy required: Live NetSuite pulls require an OAuth 1.0a signing proxy on the server side. The Data Source page validates config and stores credentials in-session. Wire a proxy URL into fetchFromNetSuite() for production use.

MES REST API Import

Pull production data live from the Manufacturing Execution System
Live API
1
Open Admin → Data Source → MES Connection

Find the MES panel beside the NetSuite connection.

2
Enter the API details

Provide the API Base URL, an API Key / Bearer Token, and the Site / Plant ID. Optionally filter to one Production Line, and choose which dataset the feed updates (Bottling, Straw, or Warehouse).

3
Test the connection

Click Test connection. The dashboard pings the endpoint with the bearer token and reports HTTP status — green if reachable and authorized.

4
Save & sync

Click Save & sync. Records are pulled live, mapped to the chosen dataset by column name, and the KPIs and charts recalculate immediately. The source pill switches to MES.

5
Try the sample feed

Click Try sample feed to pull a bundled mock endpoint live and watch a successful sync end-to-end — no real MES required.

Expected response shape
{
  "site": "PLANT-01",
  "data": [
    { "date": "2026-06-06", "line": "Line 1",
      "good_units": 4850, "total_units": 4920,
      "downtime_minutes": 35, "downtime_reason": "Changeover" }
  ]
}
The feed may return a bare JSON array or wrap rows in data, records, rows, or results. Keys are matched case-insensitively, so goodUnits, good_units, and GOOD UNITS all map correctly. CORS-enabled or plant-network endpoints sync directly; locked-down APIs route through a proxy — mapping logic stays in fetchFromMES().

Uploading Data

How to upload CSV files to populate dashboards
All Roles
1
Download a blank template

Go to Admin → Data Source → Template Upload section. Click Download next to the target dataset (Bottling, Straw, or Warehouse).

2
Fill in the template

Open the CSV in Excel or Google Sheets. Each row is one production record. Use YYYY-MM-DD format for dates. Do not rename or reorder columns.

3
Export as CSV

Save / export the file as .csv (comma-separated values). UTF-8 encoding is recommended.

4
Upload the file

In Admin → Data Source, drag the CSV onto the upload zone or click to browse. The system auto-detects the dataset type from column headers.

5
Verify the data

A success message confirms the row count and target table. The dashboard navigates to the relevant page with recalculated KPIs.

Tip: Header names are matched case-insensitively and underscores/spaces are interchangeable. For example, Good Units, good_units, and GOODUNITS all map to the same field.
